Galton Lab

Every falling ball is a coin flip. The bins pile up into the binomial distribution, and a bell curve appears on top.

  • Drop 1,000 balls at p = 0.5 and watch a bell curve grow in the bins.
  • Drag p down to 0.25 and see the stack lean left.
  • Turn on the normal curve and the 68–95–99.7 bands.

Drop balls to estimate the distribution.

Predict

Before you drop 1,000 balls at p = 0.5, sketch the shape you expect. Mark the bin you think will be tallest.

Observe

Move p from 0.5 to 0.25 and drop again. What happens to the tallest bin, and which side grows the long tail?

Explain

Repeat with 4 rows, then 16, at the same p. Why does the taller board look more like a smooth normal curve?

Formulas

P(X = k) = C(n, k) p^k (1 − p)^(n − k) μ = np σ = √(np(1 − p))

n is the number of rows, k is the bin (right turns), and p is the probability of a right turn at each peg.

Discuss

  1. A classmate says the tall middle stack proves the next ball will land there. What do the stacks actually show?
  2. You change p and keep the rows fixed. Which moves — the center, the spread, or both — and why does σ shrink as p moves away from 1/2?
  3. Pure physics uses the same pegs with the nudge turned off. Why isn’t a bell curve guaranteed anymore?
  • Engine — steps the world at a fixed 1/60 s, applies gravity, and runs sleeping.
  • Bodies — builds the pegs, balls, funnel, bin dividers, and floor.
  • Body — setVelocity is the coin-flip nudge; setPosition moves the drag anchor.
  • Composite — holds the board and removes a ball once it has settled in a bin.
  • Events — collisionStart between a ball and a peg is one Bernoulli trial.
  • Constraint — a spring from the pointer to the ball you drag.
  • Query — Query.point finds the body under the pointer.
  • Render — the X-ray wireframe pass: velocity, collisions, angle marks, and sleeping.
  • Sleeping — pure-physics balls that come to rest fall asleep, then get baked into the stack.

Each peg row is forced to be exactly one decision: on contact the ball is sent one gap left or right, plus a small wobble. Active bodies stay under a couple of hundred; settled balls are drawn as a packed stack.
